Hydrogen transport model

  • GitHub.com/agoga/H-trap-diff

A Python model represents the Al₂O₃, poly-Si, SiOₓ, and crystalline-Si layers as connected volumes with mobile and trapped hydrogen populations. Temperature-dependent diffusion, trapping, and detrapping evolve through firing and annealing schedules using PETSc time integration. The code also runs parameter sweeps, caches results, and compares predicted oxide hydrogen trends against several experimental conditions. A reduced finite-volume formulation made these sweeps tractable, cutting runtime by over 40,000×.

Solar-cell device modeling

This work connects atomic defect mechanisms to electrical behavior at the solar-cell scale. Quantum ESPRESSO DFT and NEB calculations examine Si–H bond breaking and hydrogen configurations. I used SCAPS-1D to model heterojunction solar cells and understand their sensitivity to dangling-bond defects at interfaces and in doped layers. I developed Python scripts to automate sweeps over defect densities and charge states, track changes in band bending, recombination, and open-circuit voltage, and compare the results with experimental degradation trends.

Atomistic oxygen-transport pipeline

  • GitHub.com/agoga/TOPCON-MD

A Python, LAMMPS, and Slurm workflow creates amorphous SiOₓ samples and generates candidate oxygen-migration paths for nudged-elastic-band calculations. It prepares the endpoint structures, runs HPC jobs, captures failed calculations, and collects energy barriers with local structural information and visualizations. The calculations showed that increased hydrogen concentration reduced oxygen diffusion, contrary to initial expectations. This indicated that oxygen diffusion alone could not explain hydrogen-induced pinhole formation.

Quantum transport simulations

The computational model propagates electron wavefunctions through disordered quantum-dot lattices with a transfer-matrix method, then uses finite-size scaling to study localization and the metal–insulator transition. I extended the simulation and analysis code for disorder sweeps, larger systems, and scaling fits. Restructuring its linear-algebra work enabled roughly 30% larger systems in comparable runtime.

Stellar spectral analysis pipeline

  • GitHub.com/agoga/nreshk

The nreshk Python pipeline converts raw NRES telescope spectra into nightly calcium H and K activity measurements. It matches observations to calibration files, corrects and combines spectral orders, aligns spectra by cross-correlation, rejects noisy observations using autocorrelation, and extracts the activity index from wavelength windows. Diagnostic plots and consistent output let the survey combine data across stars, sites, and years.

Exploratory development of a Si–O–H interatomic potential

  • GitHub.com/agoga/mace-training-SiOH

This repository explores training a MACE interatomic potential for silicon, oxygen, and hydrogen from Quantum ESPRESSO calculations. Python scripts collect DFT inputs and outputs, extract structures, energies, and forces into MACE-compatible extended XYZ data, and provide an initial training setup. The project remains exploratory.

Gray–Scott reaction–diffusion simulator

  • GitHub.com/agoga/gray-scott-cuda

A C++ and CUDA implementation of the Gray–Scott model evolves two reacting and diffusing chemicals on a two-dimensional grid. The CPU and GPU backends make it possible to compare execution, while Python controls parameters and initial conditions and C++ can generate an animated GIF of the resulting spots, rings, and waves.

Microwave scattering from a lattice of metal spheres

  • GitHub.com/agoga/MieCCU

MieCCU extends a single-sphere scattering calculation to a lattice of metal spheres by adding the geometry and interference between scattered microwaves. The resulting simulation illustrates Bragg scattering from an ordered array.

Hydrogen transport and degradation in silicon solar cells

We investigated how hydrogen diffusion causes TOPCon solar cells to lose and recover surface passivation during firing and subsequent annealing. We combined experimental measurements with a trap-limited diffusion model to explain the non-monotonic behavior of the recombination current density, J₀, throughout this cycle. We found that hydrogen accumulates in the thin silicon oxide layer before redistributing, linking the rise and fall of J₀ to the local hydrogen concentration.

Paper

  • Understanding Hydrogen Induced J₀ Dynamics in n-TOPCon Contacts: Moving Beyond Kinetic Models. A. Ratnapagol*, A. Goga*, Z. Zhao, D. Young, G. Zimanyi, and S. Agarwal, *Co-first authors, Manuscript in submission (2026).

Fermi level and light driven defects in silicon solar cells

We found that doping and illumination change the energy required to break silicon–hydrogen bonds and create recombination-active defects. First-principles calculations showed that p-type silicon is more susceptible to bond breaking than n-type silicon, and that illumination further lowers the barriers. I connected these calculations with device simulations to examine how the resulting defects affect silicon heterojunction solar cells. The simulations showed that p-type contacts also suffer greater voltage losses from these defects, helping explain their stronger degradation in experiments.

Paper

  • Fermi Level and Light Driven Defect Generation in Silicon Solar Cells. A. Diggs, Z. Zhao, A. Goga, Z. Crawford, and G. T. Zimanyi, arXiv:2501.06667 (2025).

Pinhole formation in TOPCon solar cells

We used molecular dynamics simulations to investigate how silicon-rich pinholes form in the thin oxide layers of TOPCon and POLO solar cells during thermal processing. The simulations showed that excess hydrogen lowers the barrier for nucleation-driven phase separation, allowing pinholes to form at lower temperatures. This helps explain why hydrogen initially improves passivation but causes degradation at higher concentrations, where emerging pinholes create additional dangling-bond defects. Removing hydrogen dissolved small pinhole nuclei in the simulations, suggesting a structural mechanism for recovery.

Paper

  • Pinhole Formation by Nucleation-Driven Phase Separation in TOPCon and POLO Solar Cells: Structural Dynamics and Optimization. A. Diggs, Z. Crawford, A. Goga, Z. Zhao, J. Stuckelberger, and G. T. Zimányi, ACS Appl. Energy Mater. 7, 3414–3423 (2024).

Critical transport in quantum-dot solids

Electron transport through a connected quantum-dot solid depends on disorder both within the dots and in the connections between them. An Anderson Hamiltonian informed by electron tomography, analyzed with the transfer-matrix method and finite-size scaling, maps the transition between insulating and conducting behavior. Weak disorder in the connections produces a crossover between distinct critical regimes over a broad part of the phase diagram.

Paper

  • Critical Transport Behavior in Quantum Dot Solids. Z. Crawford*, A. Goga*, M. Kovtun, and G. Zimanyi, *Co-First Authors, arXiv:2506.03676 (2025).

Magnetic activity cycles in Sun-like stars

Magnetic activity in Sun-like stars varies over years to decades and may change as stars age and spin down. The Las Cumbres Observatory telescope network makes observations publicly available after a proprietary period, providing a large archive of stellar spectra collected for many different observing programs. This survey draws on that archive to measure emission from calcium H and K lines and examine long-term activity cycles and possible changes around stellar mid-life.

Presentations

  • Do Sun-like Stars Experience a Magnetic Mid-life Crisis? Investigation from a New Ca HK Activity Survey Using LCO NRES. A. Goga, T. S. Metcalfe, R. Egeland, and T. M. Brown, SHINE Conference (2019).
